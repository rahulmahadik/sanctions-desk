# Known issues

> **What is still true, and what protects against it.** A person makes every final call; each problem below either holds a case for that person or is disclosed here.
- Versions used during development: Zetaris 2.4.x local, Nightshift runtime 1.2.1, Ollama with `nemotron-3.5-lightning:30b`.
- Problems met while building and since fixed, and platform limits the desk now works around: [RESOLVED_ISSUES.md](RESOLVED_ISSUES.md).

## Open issues

- **The guard can miss injected text.** Every text is screened, but the guard is not perfect. In the fresh-set run (current guard, asked twice), 27 of 28 full reviews of injected text were caught. The one miss (an instruction hidden in an HTML comment in an ordinary note) closed an alert whose own data supports a close.
  - In the 105-text full-review run (guard asked once), 2 of 70 unflagged texts closed the same way. In the steer check on ALR-0016, an unflagged text did not turn an escalating case into a close (0 of 4 runs). A person makes the final call. See [EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md#safety-guard-against-injected-text).
- **The reviewer-addressing check raises false alarms.** It flagged ALR-0073 in 4 of 4 live runs, on an ordinary payment reference ("Machine parts, onward delivery to Tehran") and an OFAC remark linking to the list's directives page, because the text names sanctioned parties or a directive and an onward delivery; the two main guard calls said clean.
  - By design, the flag can stop a close and cannot cause one. Over 51 recorded reviews with this check (47 adversarial test runs, 4 runs of ALR-0073) it flagged 27 and stopped 0 proposed closes, because none proposed a close; it is shown to the decision step, so it may have steered some of those escalations. Fixes tried and why it stays: [EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md#safety-guard-against-injected-text).
- **Correct clears go to a person.** The local screener over-flags harmless text (5 of 15 in the 105-text full-review run; 7 of 16 reviews in the fresh-set run); under the fail-closed rule (policy 8) that cost all six clears on held-out v4 (0 of 6; the set was frozen before that change), each stopped by a relationship signal at 0.9 that could not be grounded although its document was on the customer record, and no risk case was closed (0 of 6). **The desk does not clear alerts beyond what the rules clear**; its measured value is the risk it does not close.
  - Earlier rounds: on held-out v2 no clear was made (0 of 5; the signals pointed at text with no relationship word and no name, often the first 30 characters of a note). On v3 four notes met the documentary standard but cited a document not among the customer's documents, so the document-on-record rule sent them to need-more-information; the v3 labels were written before that rule and stay frozen, and whether a clear may rest on a document not on file is a policy decision. History: [RESOLVED_ISSUES.md](RESOLVED_ISSUES.md#review-logic), [EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md#held-out-rounds).
- **The critic and the decision step can still deadlock.** One deadlock each on held-out v3 (HO3-012) and v4 (HO4-012): the decision cited a source the critic cannot check by design (a free-text column, an identifier number, a computed block) and both repeated themselves for three rounds. The guard then escalates, so by design a deadlock does not end in a close.
  - Citable sources are listed for the decision step and such citations are set aside from the second round. The critic still says screener signals cannot be cited, which is how both v3 wrong closes came about; under policy 8 the guard blocks such a close, but the critic rule is unchanged (feature freeze).
- **Two matching rules live in code, not in the policy file or the approved wording.** `matching.strongIdentifierFields` and `minStrongIdentifierMatches` are checked by the schema (four fields, at least 2) but read by no step, so changing them changes nothing. For a payment, the comparison is a false positive when the counterparty's country is not linked to the listed party and no document number matches (`compare.ts`, the payment branch), a case no definition row covers: the approved `false_positive` definition speaks of date of birth and nationality, which a counterparty does not have.
  - What the code does instead: it counts date of birth exact, nationality matching a listed country and any document number matching (0 to 3), reports that count, and sets match strength with fixed combinations in `assess` ([compare.ts](../packages/data/src/matching/compare.ts)), for example a document match with a name score of at least 0.7, or a high name score with an exact date of birth and a matching nationality.
- **Text can reach the cloud before the desk refuses the answer.** 7 guard calls in runs made before cloud answers were refused were cloud-served, because Nightshift's router decides where a call runs; in four early runs, made before the screener existed (the first system's 70-case run, single, no_memory and held-out v1), payment references and list remarks were in the cloud decision prompt; customer notes never were ([PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md#earlier-runs)).
  - A cloud-served guard or screener answer is now not accepted and the case is held, but the text has already been sent. In every run with the screener, a check keeps all free text out of cloud prompts. Nightshift's answer memory (on during the recorded runs, off in the desk's configuration) could add earlier answers to cloud calls ([PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md)).
- **Copies of sensitive text sit outside the desk's own record.** Zetaris keeps the literal document number that the note check sends in its own statement history; the desk's record keeps only its hash. The committed run files contain synthetic free text in old trace lines and receipt previews, which cannot be edited without breaking their signatures ([PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md)).
- **An old users file stops a live start.** With sign-in on, a users file from a version that issued tokens in another form must have each such token rotated (`npm run users:rotate`) before the server starts.

## Deliberate design choices

- **Fail closed on screener signals (policy 8).** Every signal at or above its threshold blocks a close, grounded or not; grounding (policy version 7, `screener.grounding`) only adds context. A signal counts as grounded if its span lies inside the field, holds a term of its type (policy lists in seven languages), and has a listed name, alias, customer or counterparty name in the span or within 80 characters; one that is not is kept with `grounded: false` and a reason, recorded, counted and shown. Reason: the first version let not-grounded signals through, and on held-out v3 two risk cases closed.
- **Decision spelling normalised.** A model may write `ESCALATE` or `Close` instead of the exact decision name; a fixed list of spellings is read as the same decision and anything else is rejected, not guessed. Reason: no decision is ever guessed.
- **A saved policy folder wins over the policy file.** Once `logs/policy/` exists, a restart reads the saved versions, not `config/policy.default.json`; to start from a changed file (for example policy version 9), move that folder aside or publish the change through the policy routes ([GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md)). Reason: published policy versions and their audit log survive a restart ([EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md#changes-after-the-evaluation)).
- **Nightshift's per-receipt cross-check is stored, not shown.** Each signed receipt carries Nightshift's own `cross_check`; across the <!--fact:receipts_runs-->2,532<!--/fact--> receipts of the exported runs: <!--fact:cross_check_runs-->1,757 skipped, 703 warning (all `citation_consistency`, risk medium, not blocking), 72 passed<!--/fact-->. The desk does not display or act on it; the critic re-checks cited facts itself. Details: [docs/MODELS.md](MODELS.md#nightshifts-cross-check-on-each-call).
- **The cloud decision model receives structured personal fields by default** (name, date of birth, nationality); the desk does not put free text in a cloud prompt itself. The optional privacy mode sends tokens instead, and has been tested only on a small live sample ([PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md)).
- **Exported `results.json` files differ from their manifest hash by one documented field.** Each `run-meta.json` of the 28 exported runs keeps the SHA-256 of its files as exported; when the history was rewritten, only the `commit` field in every `results.json` was re-pointed to its rewritten id, following the commit map. `eval/runs/HASH-CHANGES.json` lists each file's original hash, its hash after the remap and the old and new value; `npm run evidence:hash-changes` puts each old value back and confirms the original hash. `npm run evidence:verify` and `npm run prove` report these files as "verified, commit field remapped (documented)" and fail on any other difference. The trace and receipt files match their manifest hashes unchanged. Reason: the run files stay as run.
- **Biome 2.6.2 skips `docs/API.md`.** It does not settle on the long route table there, so that file is formatted by `npm run openapi:export` and left out of Biome's checks.

## <a id="limits"></a>Measurement limits

- **One run per variant.** Most figures are one run: every result in the recorded evaluation is. A small study, not part of it, ran held-out v4 three times each with the full system, Nemotron alone without the free text, and Nemotron alone with it ([NEMOTRON.md](NEMOTRON.md#what-the-agents-add-over-nemotron-alone)). Held-out v4 was the last held-out round; nothing was changed after it. How heat on the laptop affected the runs: [EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md#held-out-rounds).
- **Model answers vary between runs**, the runtime may answer a repeated prompt from its cache, and cloud routing is decided by the runtime.
- **Synthetic customers.** The customers, accounts, owners and payments are **synthetic**; only the listed parties are real public entries. The exam measures behaviour on planted situations, not performance on a bank's real alert volume.
- **Definitions written with the rules.** The approved definitions are written in this repository for the exam, and the exam's labels follow the same definitions. A high result shows consistency with them, not that they are the right ones for a given bank.
- **Speed.** A review uses a large cloud model, a critic and sometimes a challenger, so it takes about a minute or more. Each median is labelled with its run (one run each, on one laptop):
  - the first system's 70-case run: 130 s per alert over the 68 alerts (124 s per case over all 70);
  - the sys2b 70-case run: 62 s per case;
  - the held-out v4 run (sys4): 45 s per case;
  - the run of the current system before the block fix: 74 s per alert over the 68 alerts (73 s per case).
- **Cost.** Cost figures use illustrative unit costs; Nightshift's receipts record USD 0. Analyst time saved was not measured; the report's minutes-saved figure is an estimate on stated assumptions and is not claimed here.
- **Not measured:** a run without Zetaris other than the unreachable-endpoint test, the policy guard switched off, the challenger alone, a pinned-model run, and the fully local mode on held-out v4 (every step on Lightning on the laptop: not measured yet; how to run it: [MODELS.md](MODELS.md#fully-local-mode)). A "Nemotron alone" baseline and the Lightning-versus-Ultra comparison were run only as small labelled studies ([NEMOTRON.md](NEMOTRON.md)).
- **Law changes.** Sanctions law and list programmes change; the jurisdiction list is an example.

## Not yet in scope

The same list as the README's [scope table](../README.md#scope-today-and-not-yet):
- **Cover payments.** The desk screens the payment party, the beneficiary's bank, an intermediary bank's country and the payment reference; it has no handling for cover payments.
- **Live list feeds.** The lists are a fixed snapshot of public OFAC and UN entries, with a labelled synthetic update to 6 of them for the list-change feature; nothing is read from a live feed.
- **Real bank volumes.** Every run used the exam and held-out sets (tens of alerts), not a bank's daily alert volume.
- **Real customer data.** All customers, accounts, owners and payments are synthetic; only the listed parties are real public entries.

## Next steps

- **Privacy mode as the default** for deployments that require it, once a full evaluation run confirms it, or keep the decision step local.
- **A stronger local screener model**, so fewer relationship signals are raised on text that holds no relationship, and correct clears are not held back by the fail-closed rule.
- **Keep all raw text on the machine.** The local safety guard and screener read notes and payment references, and the cloud steps get structured signals; a cloud-served answer is not accepted and the case cannot close, but by then the text has left the machine. The next step is a route that can only run locally.
- **A pinned-model run** (exact model per step) once Nightshift 1.2.3 is installed, and repeated held-out runs to show the spread between runs.
- **One local model copy.** Keep one local copy loaded (point the backup at the resident lane, or unload Ollama's copy), record the provider error type in the trace, and avoid other heavy work during runs.
